Trees have no brain and still remember. Not as a metaphor: as a measurable change in how they respond to the next season.

## What a tree keeps

**The same DNA, read differently.** Two Norway spruce trees with identical DNA can behave differently for years. The one whose seed developed at 18 °C starts growing much earlier in spring than its twin from a seed that developed at 28 °C [@treememories2024].

The mechanism is **epigenetic**: marks on the DNA that control how tightly it is packed, "turned off" or "turned on". The instructions are not rewritten. Which of them get used changes [@treememories2024] [@fossdal2024].

What triggers such memories in conifers:

| signal | what the tree remembers |
| --- | --- |
| temperature during seed development | when to start growing in spring |
| radiation (the trees near Chernobyl) | growth form: bushes instead of trees at high doses |
| defence chemicals applied in autumn | a stronger defence against insects the next spring |

All from the same review of conifer epigenetics [@treememories2024].

**And the scars of a population.** Plants that lived through human-caused population crashes still carry the trace generations later, as reduced genetic diversity [@mcgill2025]. Memory at the level of the species.

## Why this is fast

Natural selection rewrites the instructions. In long-lived trees that takes generations and hundreds of years. Epigenetic memory adjusts **within one lifetime**, without touching the code [@treememories2024].

That is exactly the property a working memory system needs: adapt to what happened this week without retraining anything.

## What we built from it

Our organic memory, phyto, keeps about **7,000 facts**. The facts do not change when they are used. What changes is a score on top of them, an **epigenetic score**: how often a fact was reinforced, how recently, under what stress.

| tree | phyto |
| --- | --- |
| DNA, unchanged | the fact itself, unchanged |
| epigenetic marks | the epigenetic score |
| priming by stress | hormone-like signals that raise or lower facts |
| what is expressed this season | the **aperture**: what opens for this situation |

## Where we got it backwards

Our first real test found a bug that the tree would never make. The epigenetic score was **added** to the situation's relevance instead of **gating** it. A heavily reinforced fact won every query, whatever was asked: five sticky facts held 38% of every answer.

A tree's memory does not fire in every situation. The autumn defence primes the response *to insects*. It does not make the tree defend itself against the spring. **Memory should change how a signal is answered, not replace the signal.** The fix is to gate.

## How far the analogy goes

Plants are computers in a narrower, literal sense too: a living plant's physiology has been used as a physical reservoir for computation [@pieters2022]. We do not claim our software behaves like a tree. The design rule we took is narrower and testable:

> Keep the facts fixed. Let use change how they are read. Never let use replace the question.
